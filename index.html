01

Classification · retention

2026

Complete

Telco Customer Churn

Problem
Which customers are about to cancel, and how can a probability become a retention worklist instead of a bare number?
My part
Built the whole pipeline myself: cleaning, feature engineering, stratified cross-validation of three classifiers, threshold analysis, one persisted scikit-learn Pipeline, and a Streamlit app that turns a score into a risk band and an action.
Result
Held-out test ROC-AUC 0.842 and recall 0.791: 296 of 374 churners caught. Chosen over a random forest with higher accuracy that missed more than half of them.

  • 296 / 374Churners caught (recall 0.791)
  • 7,043Customers in the dataset

Stack: Python · pandas · scikit-learn · joblib · Streamlit

2026

Complete

Olist Marketplace: Delivery & Loyalty

Problem
Across ~99,000 orders on a Brazilian marketplace, do customers come back, what drives bad reviews, and where does delivery break down?
My part
Loaded the 9 raw tables into SQLite, wrote the analysis in SQL (CTEs, window functions, cohort logic), and turned five business questions into charts, findings and recommendations.

Only 3.12% of customers ever order again. On-time orders average 4.29 stars; orders 8+ days late average 1.73. The slowest state waits 29.4 days against a 12.6-day national average.

Retail Commercial Intelligence

Problem
Turn years of item × store × day retail data into a governed model that answers three questions: what changed, why, and where should management look first?

04

Forecasting under uncertainty · Formula 1

2026

In development

Ingestion layer built and tested · forecasting not started

DPEC: Formula One Pace Intelligence

Problem
After each practice session, what can we reliably infer about Sunday’s race pace, how sure can we be, and how do we prove it afterwards?
My part
Wrote the plan, rules and architecture decisions, then built the first layer: a FastF1 ingestion CLI. It saves each session’s raw tables as Parquet with a SHA-256 manifest, skips sessions already saved, and never requests one that hasn’t happened yet.
Result
Working today: idempotent ingestion of seven raw tables per session, covered by 17 tests that run in CI on every push. Not built yet: the forecasting models, their calibration and the public ledger.
  • 7Raw tables saved per session
  • 17Tests in CI on every push
  • 0Forecast models so far (honestly)

Stack: Python 3.12 · FastF1 · pandas · PyArrow / Parquet · DuckDB · Typer · pytest · GitHub Actions

§ 02

Field notes

Short lessons taken from the projects, each with a link to the file it comes from. They are working notes, not published articles.

  1. N.01Machine learning

    Accuracy is the wrong headline for churn

    With 26.5% churners, predicting “nobody leaves” scores 73.5% accuracy and catches no one. My random forest had the best accuracy (0.788) and the worst recall (0.465). Pick the metric that prices a miss correctly.

    Source: telco · reports/findings.md ↗ (opens in a new tab)

  2. N.02Data quality

    Blank is not always missing

    Eleven blank TotalCharges values all belonged to customers with zero tenure. They are $0, not unknown. Dropping or imputing them would have invented a problem.

    Source: telco · src/preprocess.py ↗ (opens in a new tab)

  3. N.03Engineering & reproducibility

    Fitting 59 million rows on an 8 GB laptop

    Peak memory fell from 8.8 GB to 2.6 GB by streaming the reshape, using categoricals, isolating ingestion in its own process and capping DuckDB. Each change was measured on its own before keeping it.

    Source: retail · docs/architecture.md ↗ (opens in a new tab)

  4. N.04Formula 1 analytics

    Split by race weekend, never by lap

    Random lap-level splits leak the future into training. DPEC validates forward in time by race weekend, puts a naive baseline beside every model, and reports a model that loses to it as losing.

    Source: dpec · docs/rules/00-rules-and-purpose.md ↗ (opens in a new tab)

  5. N.05Open-source tools

    Parquet + DuckDB as a laptop warehouse

    Columnar files plus an embedded SQL engine replace a database server for analytical work. I made this choice in two projects and wrote it down as a decision record, rejected options included.

    Source: dpec · docs/decisions/0002-storage.md ↗ (opens in a new tab)

  6. N.06Statistics

    “Alongside”, not “because of”

    Observational retail data can show that demand fell while price rose. It can’t show that the price caused it. The metrics engine’s reporting rule is written into the code: report association, never proven causation.

    Source: retail · src/metrics.py ↗ (opens in a new tab)

  7. N.07Practical AI

    AI builds the plumbing; I build the thinking

    In DPEC, AI tools write adapters, infrastructure and scaffolding. Schema decisions, quality rules, every model and every evaluation are mine. The split is written into the project rules so it can’t drift.

    Source: dpec · rules §3, effort rules ↗ (opens in a new tab)
